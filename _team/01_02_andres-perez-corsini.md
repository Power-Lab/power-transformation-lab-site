Andrés will be joining as a postdoctoral researcher in the School of Global Policy and Strategy at the University of California San Diego. His research specializes in international trade, industrial organization, and macroeconomics, examining how trade policy, global supply chains, and market competition shape firm behavior and economic outcomes. His doctoral work investigated the competitive effects of import competition in the United States, showing that trade liberalization with China increased market competitiveness and reduced firms’ markups.

Andrés earned his Ph.D. in Economics from the University of Notre Dame. Prior to joining the School of Global Policy and Strategy, he is a Teaching Fellow at the University of Southern California, where he teaches Principles of Macroeconomics.

Before pursuing his Ph.D., Andrés worked in economic consulting in Uruguay, where he contributed to projects related to infrastructure investment, market regulation, and competition policy, supporting analysis for both public-sector and private-sector clients. His work included studies of port regulation, fuel market reforms, and the economic impacts of large-scale industrial investments.
